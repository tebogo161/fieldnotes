STUDY TOOLKIT · 3 MIN READ

A gentler, smarter way to study

A practical mix of planning, practice, shared learning and proper breaks.

By Katlego Tebogo Mpofu · Notes from my study routine

Studying can feel difficult, especially when several assignments and personal commitments compete for attention. I’m learning that a few steady habits can make it easier to get started and keep going.

Plan for the time you actually have

Keeping track of classes and deadlines helps me see what needs attention. I’m trying to plan study time alongside my other commitments, then break larger assignments into smaller tasks. A realistic plan makes a busy week feel less like one enormous to-do list.

Practise the parts that feel difficult

Repeating practice questions can help me understand challenging topics, including mathematics and computer-related concepts. Rather than only rereading, I try to work through the problem, notice where I get stuck and revisit that part.

Let other people help you learn

Working with classmates exposes me to different ways of explaining an idea. Talking a topic through can help me notice what I understand, while listening to someone else can reveal a new way to approach it.

Make room for a pause

Short breaks give me a chance to reset and return with a clearer head. I’m learning that taking a pause is part of staying focused—not a reward I have to earn after getting everything perfect.

A helpful study routine isn’t the one that looks impressive. It’s the one you can return to.

I’m still working out which habits suit me best. My aim is to stay organised, practise consistently and be patient with myself as I learn.

✳

Keep it steady. Keep it yours.
