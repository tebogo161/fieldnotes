Better together: what group work taught me

Listening, sharing the work and making room for more than one way to solve a problem.

By Katlego Tebogo Mpofu · A teamwork reflection

Group assignments have taught me that completing a task together takes more than putting several people in one group. Communication, cooperation, patience and respect all make a difference.

Different ideas are part of the process

When we first started working together, we needed to understand what the assignment asked for and get to know how each person approached it. At times, we had different opinions about how to tackle a task. I learned that there isn’t always just one useful way to think about a problem.

Instead of trying to force agreement, I found it more productive to listen, ask questions and consider the reasons behind someone else’s suggestion. That helped us move from a collection of separate ideas towards a shared approach.

Make the work visible

Sharing responsibilities gave our group a clearer way to work towards the same goal. When everyone knows what they are responsible for, it becomes easier to contribute, check progress and support each other when something gets difficult.

A strong team makes space for people to share ideas—and follows through on the work it agrees to do.

Skills I’ll carry forward

Group work has shown me that communication is an important part of teamwork. I’m practising how to share ideas clearly, respect other opinions and take ownership of the tasks I’m given. Good teamwork also asks for patience: people may contribute in different ways or need a little time to find their footing.

These lessons matter beyond one assignment. I believe cooperation and communication will help me in my studies and in the technology career I’m working towards.
