Starting college: learning to find my rhythm

New routines, bigger responsibilities, and why steady progress beats last-minute panic.

By Katlego Tebogo Mpofu · A first-year reflection

Starting at Botswana Accountancy College has been exciting. As a first-year Computer Systems Engineering student, I’m learning new things, meeting new people and adjusting to a different learning environment.

More responsibility, more independence

Moving from secondary school to college has made one thing clear: I have to take a more active role in my education. Class is an important starting point, but it can’t be the only place I learn. I need to revisit ideas, practise what I’ve covered and take responsibility for getting assignments done.

That change hasn’t always felt easy. Balancing assignments, study time and personal commitments can be difficult. I’m learning to plan ahead and break work into smaller steps, rather than waiting until a deadline is close and trying to do everything at once.

Not understanding something straight away doesn’t mean I can’t learn it. It means I have a reason to ask, practise and try again.

Progress is a practice

Some of the computer and technology topics I’m meeting are completely new to me. I’m discovering that a positive attitude matters: challenging topics are not proof that I can’t do the work. They’re an invitation to slow down, ask questions and build understanding piece by piece.

Assignments are teaching me more than course content, too. They’re helping me practise discipline, patience and following through on a task. I’m proud of the progress I’ve made, while recognising there’s still a lot to learn—especially when it comes to practical computer skills.

Where I’m heading

I want to grow my technical knowledge, become more confident solving problems and prepare for the working world. My goal is to become a skilled Computer Systems Engineer and use what I learn to make a positive contribution to society.

For now, every lesson, assignment and challenge is one more step. I don’t need to have everything figured out today; I just need to keep showing up and learning.

✳

One step at a time.
